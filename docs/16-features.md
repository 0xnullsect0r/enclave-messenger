Status: Draft (M0) · Normative

Source: PLAN.md §14 (feature parity), §20 (milestones). This matrix fixes which features ship in which milestone and the privacy rule each must follow. A feature marked for a milestone is part of that milestone's exit gate (M9: "parity rows up to M9 complete").

## 3. As implemented: locations

`enclave-core/src/client/location.rs`. A location is `i32 lat ‖ i32 lon` in millionths of a degree (about 11 cm) and a label of at most 200 B, sent as `Content::Location { id, location, expires }` (content kind 20) with the conversation's disappearing timer. Receivers reject coordinates off the Earth. The place is stored inside the message record (`Message::location`), so it is sealed with the message and crypto-shredded with it when the timer runs out; deleting for everyone removes it too. Nothing is looked up on either side: no map tiles, geocoding or place names, and no network request at all. The app takes typed or pasted coordinates from the attach sheet and shows the label, the coordinates in the monospace face, **Copy coordinates** and **Open in maps**. On phones **Use my location** fills the coordinates from one fix, asked for when tapped and never in the background: Android reads its GPS provider only (`LocationManager`, permission `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`), never a network location service, and iOS uses Core Location (when-in-use). **Open in maps** first says that the maps app may look the place up online, then hands it over: a `geo:` link in Android's chooser, a maps link in iOS's share sheet, `geo:` through `xdg-open` on Linux, `maps://` on macOS and `bingmaps:` on Windows, so the person picks the app. Enclave itself still fetches nothing. Test `share_a_location`.

**Encrypted profile, as implemented.** `enclave-core/src/client/profile.rs`. Our profile is the display name, "about" (at most `MAX_ABOUT`, 280 B) and an optional photo, kept in the synced setting `profile` so our other devices have it too, with a version that grows with every change. It goes only to accepted contacts, inside their sessions, as `Content::Profile { name, about, photo, version }` (content kind 38, protobuf field 38): never to a server, a card, an invite or a group. Upkeep sends the current version to at most 8 contacts a round (`profile-sent/<root>` records what each has), so a change doesn't burst out at once and a contact accepted later gets it too. The photo is redrawn at 512 px in mediad (metadata gone), sealed like any attachment (`08-envelope.md` §9) on our own server, and the profile carries its reference; our copy is kept, and a photo older than 25 days (`PHOTO_REFRESH_SECS`, inside the server's 30-day chunk lifetime) is uploaded again before the next send. A received profile is kept only from an accepted contact and only if its version is newer (`profile-of/<root>`), so a replayed copy can't put an older one back. Contacts' photos are fetched after a random delay (`fetch_due`, RT-17) and shown at 160 px in place of the contact art, in the list, the header, the members sheet and the conversation sheet, which also shows their "about". Test `profiles_reach_contacts_only`.

**Stories, as implemented.** `enclave-core/src/client/stories.rs`. A story is a few words (at most 700 B) and/or a picture or clip, shown for 24 hours (`STORY_SECS`). It goes to each person in its audience inside their own session as `Content::Story { id, text, attachment, age, gone }` (content kind 39, protobuf field 39), never through a group, so viewers don't learn about each other; upkeep sends it to 8 contacts a round, and `age` lets the receiver's 24 hours end with the poster's. A picture is re-encoded in mediad (a GIF becomes an AV1 clip) and sealed on the poster's server like any attachment; a viewer fetches it when opening the story, decoded in mediad. The audience is every accepted contact who isn't blocked or hidden (**Your story → Who sees it**; the list stays on the device, setting `story-hidden`). Viewing sends nothing back. Deleting a story sends `gone` to those it reached; our other devices get our stories and deletions as self-copies. A receiver keeps at most 30 from one contact, only from accepted, unblocked contacts, and drops every story at its 24 hours. Tests `stories_reach_the_audience_for_a_day` and `stories_through_the_engine`.

## 4. As implemented: media gallery

Local only, in the app (`enclave-app/ui/app.slint`, sheet `gallery`): the conversation sheet offers **Photos and files (n)**, a list of every attachment in the open conversation that hasn't been deleted, with the decoded preview for pictures (from `mediad`, as in the conversation) and **Save**. It is built from the messages the vault already sends the UI. Groups have the same list (07 §7.6). **All conversations** (a chip in the same sheet, and Settings → **Photos and files from every conversation**) lists every file in every conversation, newest first, with its conversation's name: the vault reads it from the sealed store on request (`Client::all_files`, `Cmd::AllFiles` → `Out::Gallery`, at most 2,000 rows); pictures open in the viewer and anything can be saved. Nothing is downloaded until it is opened or saved; stickers and view-once files are never listed.

## 5. As implemented: stickers

`enclave-core/src/client/stickers.rs`. A pack is a title (at most 64 B) and 1 to 40 pictures, encoded `"ESP1" ‖ bytes(title) ‖ u8 n ‖ n × bytes(picture)` (at most 512 KiB each, 8 MiB in all), and sealed like any file (`08-envelope.md` §9): chunks padded to a size bucket at random IDs on its creator's server, under a key only holders of the reference have. That reference is the "pack secret". The app redraws each picture in `mediad` first (`MediaOp::Shrink`: at most 512 px a side, metadata gone). Sending a sticker sends the reference and an index: `Content::Sticker` (content kind 22, with the conversation's timer) or, in a group, `FLAG_RICH` kind 9. The server sees an ordinary unit. A recipient downloads the whole pack once (every chunk of its bucket, hash-checked), decodes the one picture in `mediad`, and can **Add pack**; added packs are listed per device. The app's sticker button opens a picker of added packs (thumbnails decoded in `mediad`) and **Make a pack** from picture files. **Animated stickers**: a GIF given to **Make a pack** becomes a short AV1 clip in `mediad` (as an animated picture does, `15-client.md` §5.3), at most 512 KiB; it shows its first frame in the picker and the conversation and plays in the viewer when tapped, decoded in `mediad` like any clip. **See the pack** on a received sticker downloads the pack once and shows its title, how many stickers and each picture (decoded in `mediad`) before **Add pack**. Each added pack has **Remove**, which forgets it on this device (the sealed chunks expire on their server). Tests `sticker_packs`, `sticker_pack_through_the_engine` and `animated_stickers_pack_preview_and_removal`.

## 7. As implemented: typing indicators

`enclave-core/src/client/typing.rs`; 1:1 conversations and groups.

- **Off by default, and both ways**: Settings → Typing indicators. With them off we send none and show none.
- **Wire**: `Content::Typing { on }` (content kind 23), sealed like any message. The spec said typing would ride in `Piggyback`, inside a real message's padding, but you type *before* there is a message to ride on, so that can't work. Instead each indicator is its own unit sent with `Transport::exchange_droppable`: it may take only a slot of the next two ticks that would otherwise carry cover, and is dropped if none is free. The pattern on the wire is the same with typing indicators on or off, which is what "never its own unit" was protecting.
- **What a dropped one costs**: the write token it spent (the contact's server never sees it), and one skipped message key on the receiving side, which the ratchet already handles as a lost message. It carries **no PQ slot**, so a dropped unit never loses a PQ step, and **no self-copy**, so our other devices don't learn we were typing.
- **Tokens**: none is sent while the contact has given us `TYPING_RESERVE` (8) write tokens or fewer, so real messages always have tokens left.
- **Timing**: the app sends "typing" when the draft becomes non-empty and repeats it at most every 5 s while the person types, and sends "stopped" when the draft is cleared. A message itself ends the indicator. The receiving app shows "typing…" under the name for `TYPING_SHOW_SECS` (10 s) after the last one, since a "stopped" may never arrive. The sending is detached from the vault's command loop, so waiting for a slot never delays anything else.
- **Groups**: an indicator is an *ephemeral* group message (`enclave_proto::group::FLAG_EPHEMERAL`, inside the encrypted sender header): to the server an ordinary 14,336 B group unit in the day's mailbox, sent the same droppable way. Its body is sealed under `KMAC256(epoch secret, nonce, "enclave/v1/proto/grp-ephemeral")` rather than a step of the sender chain, and it carries counter `0xFFFFFFFF`, so neither side's chain or frontier moves and a dropped one leaves no gap for members to be warned about (RT-24); the MAC vector authenticates it like any message. It holds `1 ‖ on ‖ u64 time`, and members ignore one more than 30 s off their clock (a replay by the server). A member who hasn't posted in the group yet has no sender chain and sends none (starting one is a rekey). The app shows "Ana, Ben typing…" under the group's name. Test `group_typing_indicators`, and `ephemeral_messages_take_no_step` in `enclave-proto`.

Test `typing_indicators` (`crates/enclave-core/tests/client.rs`): off by default, both ways; on and off arrive in order; with a transport that drops every droppable request, the conversation continues normally afterwards; blocked contacts get none. Screenshots 47–48.
